Introduction

What is AGH-CSMS?

AGH-CSMS is the digital system Adare General Hospital uses to manage daily clinical operations. Every nurse, doctor, and department head uses it every day.

🏥

Why Does Adare General Hospital Use This System?

Before this system, staff check-in was done on paper. Shift handovers were口头 (verbal). Records were scattered. This system solves all of that:

  • 1Digital check-in/out — Every staff member clocks in and out with a personal 4-digit PIN. The hospital knows exactly who is on duty at any moment.
  • 2ISBAR clinical reports — Standardized handover reports ensure nothing is missed when a patient is transferred between shifts.
  • 3Staff scheduling — Department heads plan weekly shifts digitally. Staff can see their schedule anytime.
  • 4Patient records — All clinical data is stored in one place, searchable by MRN or patient name.
  • 5Real-time visibility — Administrators see hospital-wide status: who is on duty, how many patients, resource availability.

📱 What You Need

  • ✓A computer, tablet, or smartphone with a web browser (Chrome recommended)
  • ✓Internet connection (hospital WiFi)
  • ✓Your personal username and password (given by your department head or admin)
  • ✓Your 4-digit personal PIN (set by your department head — you must remember this)

⚠️ Important for All Staff

Your PIN is how the system knows it is you. Never share your PIN with anyone. If you forget your PIN, ask your department head to reset it for you (see Section 8).

Logging In

Step-by-step guide to access the system for the first time

Step-by-Step

How to Log In — Detailed Walkthrough

1

Open Your Web Browser

On your computer, open Google Chrome (recommended). On your phone, open Chrome or Safari.

💡 Tip: Bookmark the login page so you can find it quickly next time.
2

Type the Hospital URL

In the address bar at the top, type the web address given to you by IT. It looks something like: http://192.168.x.x:5173 or the hospital domain.

💡 Tip: Do NOT search for it on Google. Type the address directly.
3

See the Login Screen

You will see a blue screen with the hospital logo and two fields: Username and Password.

4

Enter Your Username

Type the username your department head gave you. This is usually your name or a short code (e.g., melese.t).

💡 Usernames are case-sensitive — type exactly as given.
5

Enter Your Password

Type the password your department head gave you. The characters will show as dots (••••) for security.

⚠️ If you get "Invalid credentials" — check caps lock is off, and retype carefully.
6

Click "Login"

Click the blue Login button. If your username and password are correct, you will see the Dashboard.

💡 The first time you log in, your department head will guide you through the system.

You MUST check in at the start of every shift and check out at the end. This tracks your attendance.

Common Problem

"I Forgot My PIN!" — What To Do

This happens to everyone. Here is exactly what to do.

📋 Scenario: You Forgot Your PIN

It is 7:00 AM. You arrive at work and try to clock in. The system asks for your PIN. You cannot remember it. What do you do?

1

Do NOT Panic

Your department head can reset your PIN in less than 1 minute. This is a normal, everyday occurrence.

2

Find Your Department Head

Walk to your ward manager or senior nurse (your department head). Tell them: "I forgot my PIN, can you reset it please?"

3

They Will Reset It

Your department head goes to User Management, finds your name, enters their OWN password for security, then sets a new PIN for you.

4

You Get Your New PIN

Your department head tells you the new PIN. Write it down somewhere safe or save it in your phone.

💡 Tip: Use a simple pattern you can remember, like your birth year digits or a lucky number.

🛡️ Why Does the Department Head Need Their Password?

This is a security feature. It prevents anyone from resetting your PIN without permission. Only your direct department head — the person who manages you — can reset your PIN.

  • ✓Admin can reset ANYONE's PIN
  • ✓Department head can reset ONLY their own staff's PINs
  • ✗Other staff CANNOT reset your PIN
  • ✗You CANNOT reset your own PIN

📌 For Department Heads: How to Reset a Staff PIN

  • 1Go to Admin → User Management
  • 2Find the staff member in the list
  • 3Click "Set Access PIN" or "Reset"
  • 4Enter YOUR password (security check)
  • 5Enter the new 4-digit PIN for the staff
  • 6Click Save PIN

ISBAR Clinical Reports

How to write structured handover reports for patient care

Clinical Reporting

Writing an ISBAR Report — Complete Guide

ISBAR is a structured way to communicate patient information during handovers. Every nurse and doctor at Adare General Hospital must know this format.

I — Identify

Who is the patient?

Patient full name, MRN (Medical Record Number), age, gender, which ward/department they are in, and who you are (your name and role).

Example: "This is Nurse Melese. I am calling about Mr. Ahmed Hassan, MRN 12345, male, 65 years old, in ICU Bed 3."

S — Situation

What is happening right now?

The current problem. Why are you calling or handing over? What is the patient's main complaint today?

Example: "Mr. Ahmed's oxygen levels have dropped to 88% in the last 2 hours despite being on 4L nasal cannula."

B — Background

What is the patient's history?

Relevant medical history, allergies, current medications, recent surgeries, why they were admitted, and any important past events.

Example: "Mr. Ahmed was admitted 3 days ago for pneumonia. He is diabetic (Type 2), allergic to penicillin, and is on Metformin, Amoxicillin, and supplemental oxygen."

A — Assessment

What do you think is happening?

Your clinical findings — vital signs (BP, HR, Temp, SpO2, RR), physical exam findings, lab results, and your professional judgment.

Example: "Current vitals: BP 130/80, HR 95, Temp 38.2°C, SpO2 88%, RR 24. I am concerned his pneumonia is worsening. His chest sounds diminished on the right side."

R — Recommendation

What should happen next?

Your suggested plan of action, what you need from the doctor, time frames, and any immediate interventions needed.

Example: "I recommend increasing oxygen to 6L, ordering a chest X-ray, and reviewing antibiotics. Can you please come assess within 30 minutes?"

📝 How to Submit an ISBAR Report

1

Click "Report" in the sidebar

2

Select the patient (or create a new one)

3

Fill in each ISBAR section (I, S, B, A, R)

4

Click "Submit"

✓

Report is saved. You can find it later in "All Records"

💡 Tips for Good ISBAR Reports

  • Be specific — say "SpO2 is 88%" not "oxygen is low"
  • Include exact numbers for vital signs
  • Mention time frames — "in the last 2 hours"
  • State your concern level — "I am worried because..."
  • Give a clear recommendation — "Please come within 30 minutes"
  • Use the patient's MRN — never rely on name alone

Resources — What to Know

The Resources page shows your department's staffing levels at a glance. This helps you know if you are short-staffed.

  • ●Green — Sufficient staff available
  • ●Amber — Staffing is borderline, may need support
  • ●Red — critically short-staffed, alert admin

How to use: Check Resources at the start of your shift to see how many nurses and midwives are available in your ward. If staffing is red, notify your department head immediately.

The "All Records" page lets you search and browse all submitted ISBAR reports.

  • 1Search by MRN — Type the patient's Medical Record Number

  • 5Click any record to see the full ISBAR details
